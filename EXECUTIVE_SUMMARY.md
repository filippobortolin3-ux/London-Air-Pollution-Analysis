# London clean-air intervention priorities

## Decision question

Which London boroughs should receive priority for further clean-air assessment when both exposure severity and the scale of affected populations and vulnerable sites are considered?

## Main result

The baseline model ranks Westminster, Tower Hamlets, Camden, Southwark and Hackney as the five highest-priority boroughs. These areas combine high exposure rates with substantial numbers of affected residents, schools, hospitals and care homes.

| Rank | Borough | Priority score |
|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | Westminster | 89.6 |
| 2 | Tower Hamlets | 79.1 |
| 3 | Camden | 78.4 |
| 4 | Southwark | 71.4 |
| 5 | Hackney | 69.8 |

The ranking is broadly stable when the balance between severity and scale changes. The Spearman correlation between the baseline ranking and each alternative ranking exceeds 0.97. Some individual areas are weight-sensitive. City of London moves from fourth under the severity-focused scenario to twenty-second under the scale-focused scenario because exposure is intense but the resident population and number of vulnerable sites are relatively small.

## Recommended use

Use the ranking as a screening tool rather than an automatic funding formula.

1. Begin detailed local assessment in Tier 1 boroughs.
2. Identify dominant emission sources before selecting interventions.
3. Prioritise monitoring and protective measures around schools, hospitals and care homes.
4. Add intervention cost, deprivation and implementation capacity before making final funding decisions.

Potential measures include school-street interventions, targeted roadside monitoring, low-emission transport initiatives and site-specific protection around health facilities. The current dataset identifies where exposure is concentrated but does not establish which measure would be most effective.

## Method

The score combines two equally weighted dimensions:

- **severity**, measured using exposure rates above 20 µg/m³ annual NO₂ and 10 µg/m³ annual PM2.5;
- **scale**, measured using affected counts, normalised across boroughs.

Within each dimension, population receives 40% weight, schools 30% and hospitals/care homes 30%. Alternative scenarios assign 70% or 30% weight to severity.

## Limitations

- The analysis uses modelled annual concentrations for 2022.
- Threshold and weight choices are normative assumptions.
- Exposure counts for different pollutants overlap and are averaged, not added as unique people or sites.
- The model does not estimate causal effects or intervention benefits.
- Final policy design requires source attribution, costs and distributional analysis.
